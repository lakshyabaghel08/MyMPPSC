# 2012 Paper I — independent answer audit, batch 1

This is a **staging audit**, not an official MPPSC key and not an app-facing
bank. The underlying paper transcription and original generator scripts are
preserved unchanged alongside this file. Answers below are independently
reasoned from the cited sources, not accepted because the recovered script
claims verification. Options are identified by the labels in the supplied
2012 transcription. Dates in source materials are considered in context;
modern reports do not prove what the commission's key said in 2012. No official
key has been inspected. Other questions are **not independently audited here**.

## Supported answers

### Q1 — B, buffalo (unofficial)

A 2009 news report quoting the NDRI team describes Garima as its second cloned
buffalo calf. A sheep (A), a cow (C), and a goat (D) do not match that report;
the fact that cloning exists in another species does not make it the NDRI
achievement asked about. The source is a contemporary secondary report, not an
official examination key or a primary laboratory publication.
[Financial Express, 26 July 2009](https://www.financialexpress.com/archive/cloning-glory/494395/).

### Q2 — D, python (unofficial)

Pythons are nonvenomous constrictors. Cobras (A) and coral snakes (C) are
venomous elapids, while vipers (B) are venomous viperids. The paper says
"poisonous"; venomous is the precise term for venom delivered by a bite.
Sources: [Britannica, python](https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/python/313104),
[WHO snakebite review](https://www.emro.who.int/in-press/reviews/global-prevalence-of-neurotoxic-and-hemotoxic-snakebite-envenomation-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis.html).

### Q3 — B, 1973 (unofficial)

The National Tiger Conservation Authority dates the launch to April 1973.
1971 (A), 1977 (C), and 1991 (D) therefore are not the launch year. The date
is an institutional fact, not dependent on a retrospective answer key.
[NTCA, Project Tiger](https://ntca.gov.in/reports/).

### Q5 — A, virus and female Aedes mosquito (unofficial)

Dengue is caused by dengue virus and generally transmitted by the bite of an
infected female Aedes mosquito. B incorrectly says bacterium and Culex; C
incorrectly says fungus, despite naming Aedes; D incorrectly says protozoan
and Anopheles. Other mosquito-borne diseases should not be substituted for
dengue. [WHO dengue fact sheet](https://apps.who.int/mediacentre/factsheets/fs117/en/).

### Q6 — D, oxygen-carrying capacity of blood (unofficial)

Carbon monoxide binds haemoglobin, producing carboxyhaemoglobin and reducing
blood's ability to carry oxygen. A (digestion), B (liver function), and C
(kidney function) are not the principal blood-transport mechanism asked for;
this does not imply that organs cannot suffer secondary injury from hypoxia.
[CDC, carbon monoxide poisoning](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/preview/mmwrhtml/00000493.htm).

### Q7 — B, state of rest (unofficial)

Statics studies forces in equilibrium, in the elementary formulation bodies
at rest. Motion (A) is the concern of dynamics/kinematics rather than the
intended contrast; state of mind (C) is psychology; data (D) is statistics,
not statics. Equilibrium is more precise than the shorthand "rest".
[Britannica, statics](https://www.britannica.com/science/statics).

### Q8 — C, Pluto (unofficial)

The IAU's 2006 resolution separates planets and dwarf planets and explicitly
classifies Pluto as a dwarf planet. Uranus (A), Neptune (B), and Jupiter (D)
are listed among the eight planets. "Dwarf planet" is not a subcategory of
planet under that resolution. [IAU 2006 resolution results](https://www.iau.org/IAU/Iau/News/PR2006/iau-2006-general-assembly-resolution-votes.aspx).

### Q9 — D, 1 December (unofficial)

WHO states that World AIDS Day is observed on 1 December each year. The
other proposed dates—12 January (A), 1 April (B), and 1 May (C)—are not that
annual observance. [WHO World AIDS Day](https://www.emro.who.int/world-aids-campaigns/wad2016/overview.html).

### Q10 — D, NHPC (unofficial)

NHPC is a hydroelectric power generator; a contemporaneous company-hosted
credit analysis describes it as a hydroelectric generation utility. MMTC (A)
is a trading corporation, MTNL (B) a telecommunications company, and NCL
(C) a coal producer: none is the electricity-generating organisation the
question seeks. [NHPC-hosted credit analysis, 2012](https://www.nhpcindia.com/assests/pzi_public/pdf_link/6478686332fd3.pdf).

### Q11 — C, Jataka stories (unofficial)

A conservation report for the Ajanta World Heritage property explicitly
identifies murals representing Jataka stories and other Buddhist religious
subjects. The Ramayana (A), Mahabharata (B), and Panchatantra (D) are distinct
literary traditions and are not the documented Jataka association requested.
[UNESCO World Heritage Centre conservation report](https://whc.unesco.org/document/162845).

### Q13 — B, Buddhists (unofficial)

Tripitaka is the Sanskrit name for the Buddhist "three baskets" of canonical
texts, including monastic discipline and discourses. Jain (A), Sikh (C), and
Hindu (D) traditions have their own scriptures; Tripitaka is the Buddhist
canon asked about, not the canonical designation for those traditions.
[Britannica, Pali canon](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Tipitaka).

### Q14 — C, history of Kashmir (unofficial)

Kalhana's Rajatarangini is a verse chronicle of Kashmir, blending earlier
legends with historical narratives. It is not primarily Chandragupta's reign
(A), an anthology of lyrics (B), or Krishna Deva Raya's reign (D).
[Britannica, Rajatarangini](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Rajatarangini).

### Q34 — C, Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs (unofficial; recovered key conflicts)

The ministry's handbook states that work relating to constitution and working
of the zonal railway informal consultative committees was assigned to it.
Thus the President (A), Ministry of Railways (B), and Ministry of Transport
(D) do not match the named responsibility. The recovered B and explanation
are contradicted by this institutional source. No official paper key has been
consulted. [Ministry of Parliamentary Affairs handbook](https://www.mpa.gov.in/sites/default/files/parlia1.pdf).

### Q100 — A, speedy trial (unofficial; recovered key conflicts)

The original section 14 specifies a Court of Session as a Special Court "for
the purpose of providing for speedy trial". B, a time-bound trial, and C,
special protection for victims, are not listed as purposes in that provision;
D (all three) therefore does not follow from it. Later amendments must not be
assumed to have applied at the 2012 exam date. The recovered D and its claim
that section 14 specified all three purposes conflict with the original text.
[India Code, original 1989 Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/16874/1/sc_st_(preventionofatrocities)_act_1989.pdf).

## Explicitly unresolved

### Q4 — likely C, wording conflict

The Hindi option C says wind velocity, which matches an anemometer; the English
option says "Force of the wind", not speed. NOAA defines an anemometer as
measuring wind **speed**, not force. A (water-flow velocity), B (water depth),
and D (light intensity) are not the intended measurement. Do not silently
change the English source wording or represent the translations as exactly
equivalent; retain this language discrepancy and review against the original
paper and official key if available. [NOAA glossary](https://forecast.weather.gov/glossary.php?word=anemometer).

### Q15 — ambiguous: C and D both appear defensible

The paper asks who was an advocate at the famous INA trials, not who was the
chief advocate. Both Asaf Ali (C) and Bhulabhai Desai (D) were in the defence
team; Desai led it. Accordingly D may be the intended answer if "chief" was
meant, but the wording does not exclude C. A (Subhash Chandra Bose) was the
INA leader, not counsel in the post-war trial. B (C. Rajagopalachari) has not
been established as a member of this defence team in the cited source; do not
infer more than that. Flag rather than force a single key. [LiveLaw discussion of the Red Fort trials](https://www.livelaw.in/articles/two-monumental-red-fort-trials-bahadur-shah-zafar-ina-301090).
